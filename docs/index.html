Introduction

Duolingo is the world’s most popular language learning app. Founded by Luis von Ahn at Carnegie Mellon University in 2011, the app has since grown to hundreds of millions of active users. The app offers free, engaging language mini-lessons while gamifying features to build daily habits. Through bite-sized lessons, the app breaks down unfamiliar words and concepts using research-backed methods like spaced repetition, active retrieval, and adaptive difficulty. While the app originally started for language learning, it has since expanded to include subjects like math, music, and chess. Beyond a fun and interactive user interface, Duolingo works towards a broader mission of making high-quality language education accessible to anyone with a smartphone, regardless of socioeconomic status. Using a dataset of student learning traces in the app, we can better understand how users interact with the app, whether lessons are effective, and what user characteristics are. Ultimately, our goal is to explore how usage of the Duolingo app is related to language learning.

Data

The original dataset contains 13 million student learning traces (lessons) from Duolingo, a popular language learning app. The dataset’s source is a paper published by Duolingo researchers in 2016. Our analysis set will include 10,000 randomized rows. The app uses a spaced repetition approach for users to reinforce word learning. Each line in this file represents one Duolingo lesson with a target word to practice.

  • Source: Replication Data for: A Trainable Spaced Repetition Model for Language Learning
  • Size: 5000 rows × 12 columns (after cleaning)
  • Key attributes: learning language, history seen, history correct

References

  1. The effectiveness of Duolingo in developing receptive and productive language knowledge and proficiency. Smith, B., Jiang, X., & Peters, R. (2024) studied 48 English speakers learning Spanish for three months using Duolingo. The participants significantly improved on every skill tested, including reading, writing, listening, and speaking, supporting the notion that language learning apps can enhance a range of language skills.
  2. The effectiveness of app-based and classroom-based instruction on L2 learning and motivation. González-Fernández, B. & de la Viña, I. (2025) studied 337 Spanish speakers learning English over 16 weeks, either in a classroom or using Duolingo. Both groups improved by the end of the period. Duolingo learners did better in general proficiency and vocabulary recognition, while classroom learners improved more in listening. Both groups remained motivated, with Duolingo learners reporting slightly higher interest in their course.
